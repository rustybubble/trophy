## Ownership and evidence boundary

Perception owns source frames, source and session timestamps, camera transforms, ball/hand observations, uncertainty bounds and candidate events. The referee/engine owns point/let decisions, score, service order, corrections and explanations. Platform owns authoritative shared-state publication and speech. The standalone `services/flight/referee.py` is an engine handoff used by the recorded demo server; it is isolated from the detector and does not amend shared contracts or replace the production engine service.

Use stable event and rally IDs. Two views of one rally are evidence for one outcome, not two points. The recorder's padded clip boundaries are not the legally defined in-play interval. A disappearance or direction change is a candidate event; it cannot identify a winner by itself. Reference avatars, wrists, paddles and interpolation never supply missing evidence. No probability of a correct ruling is inferred from a detector heatmap.

Validated rule triggers include source-frame references and observed actor/team identity. Missing, conflicting, uncalibrated or unsupported measurements result in `review_needed`, with the score unchanged. The current generic evidence adapter emits BETA with no probability even for supported triggers: its rule facts are deterministic, but no calibrated correctness probability exists. A future production confidence gate may grant AUTO only after validating its actual measurement pipeline. Explicit player actions are labeled `source: manual` and audited. Recreational player resolution is not represented as an official ITTF appeal or as automatic visual adjudication.

## Replay, challenge and score audit

Challenge opens synchronized sources and freezes the decisive interval; it does not silently create or reverse a point. An inconclusive review retains an existing decision. When there was never a supported original decision, the point remains pending. A player/judge can resolve or correct a point through the backend; the correction supersedes the old event ID and recomputes totals and service state once. The history retains both decisions. Seeking/replaying the same point has no scoring side effect.

The demo's manual resolution is a recreational control. Formal official TTR operates under 3.2.7.1, 3.3.2.8 and 3.3.3.3, with final decision by the TTR official. Ordinary factual appeals and interpretation appeals have different authorities under 3.3.3; do not imply any player agreement can override a formal official's factual decision. 3.4.1 says call score after the rally, next server's score first (game winner first at game end); platform speech should consume committed state. Practice, intervals/timeouts and suspension provisions in 3.4.2–.4 require official input. The demo does not automate these administrative regulations.

## Executable acceptance and remaining validation

`tests/test_referee.py` contains 24 synthetic-fact acceptance scenarios: supported point adapters and citations; ordinary and deuce scoring; two-point/single-point service; lets and prerequisites; net-on-return distinction; match progression; final-game ends; duplicate same-camera/multi-camera outcomes; manual resolutions; corrections/rollback; conflicting/missing evidence; incomplete recordings; order correction preserving prior points; unsupported doubles/accessibility; invalid IDs/settings; threshold equality/uncertainty and metric-validation gates. These validate state behavior, **not phone-video detector accuracy**.

Full rule coverage is not claimed. In particular, official doubtful-service warnings, doubles individual order, wheelchair-specific sensors, expedite timing/13-return counters, equipment tests and discipline are inventoried with explicit manual/unsupported status. Test scenarios for them verify safe deferral where applicable, not full enforcement. No real clip score, height, angle, edge or open-palm accuracy has been established by these tests.
